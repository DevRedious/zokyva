## 1. What is a Brand Neologism?

A **neologism** *(from Ancient Greek néos "new" and lógos "word")* is an entirely coined word that **did not previously exist in the dictionary**.

In the world of tech startups, SaaS, holding companies, and modern business, a neologism is the ultimate differentiation asset. It stands in stark contrast to generic or common dictionary words (such as *Apple*, which cost millions in legal battles against the Beatles' record label, or *Booking*, which is notoriously difficult to defend as an exclusive trademark).

### The 4 Major Families of Neologisms:
1. **Purely Coined Words (Total Invention)**: Short, distinctive, and punchy sounds with no prior root.
   * *Famous examples*: **Rolex**, **Kodak**, **Xerox**.
2. **Portmanteau Words (Blends)**: Intelligent fusion of two complementary concepts.
   * *Famous examples*: **Spotify** (*Spot* + *Identify*), **Instagram** (*Instant* + *Telegram*), **Microsoft** (*Microcomputer* + *Software*).
3. **Morphological Alterations**: Stylized modifications of scientific or mathematical terms.
   * *Famous examples*: **Google** (derived from *Googol*, 1 followed by 100 zeros), **Flickr**, **Lyft**.
4. **Identity & Ecosystem Neologisms**: Words coined to embody an entire ecosystem or technical universe.
   * *Examples*: **Redious**, **Zokyva**, **Wilobot**.
